Project Jupyter is committed to making its software and community resources
accessible. Jupyter is an ecosystem of independently maintained subprojects,
each with its own user interfaces and accessibility work. Accessibility varies
by project and feature, and there is still significant work to do.

This page directs you to accessibility information for Jupyter
projects and ways to participate in improving accessibility in Project Jupyter.

## Accessibility Subproject

The [Jupyter Accessibility Subproject](https://jupyter-accessibility.readthedocs.io/en/latest/)
provides cross-project guidance and strategy for making Jupyter's core
user-facing software and related tools accessible. Its
[accessibility documentation](https://jupyter-accessibility.readthedocs.io/en/latest/accessibility-docs.html)
includes resources, guidance, and notes on completed work.

## Get involved

Join the [accessibility category on the Jupyter Discourse forum](https://discourse.jupyter.org/c/special-topics/accessibility)
or [Jupyter Zulip #accessibility channel](https://jupyter.zulipchat.com/#narrow/channel/644830-accessibility) to ask questions and share
ideas. The Accessibility and Jupyter Frontends
subprojects also hold [weekly community meetings](https://github.com/jupyter/accessibility#join-our-accessibility-meetings).
You can follow and contribute to work in the
[Jupyter Accessibility GitHub repository](https://github.com/jupyter/accessibility).

## Project-specific information

Accessibility status and documentation are maintained by individual projects.
For more detailed information, see:

- [JupyterLab accessibility statement](https://jupyter-accessibility.readthedocs.io/en/latest/resources/JupyterLab-a11y-statement.html)
- [Jupyter Book accessibility](https://jupyterbook.org/accessibility/)
